## About Talona

Talona is an AI agent that connects to existing data sources and flags issues in real time, before they turn into rework and delays. It is built for small and mid-size general contractors building multifamily housing. Superintendents use it every day on site.

## The Problem

The current state. Construction is one of the oldest industries in the world and one of the last to go digital. A building site turns into a different place every week. The site runs on group chats, email threads and spreadsheets, and decisions made there often never reach the people who need them in time.

Why it matters. Information gets lost or goes stale before it reaches the crew. One crew closes a wall before the inspection. Another builds from last week’s drawings. Each slip turns into rework and delays. Bad data and miscommunication cost the global construction industry an estimated $1.85 trillion in 2020.

Who is affected. General contractors, the superintendents running the site, and the crews building from information that may already be out of date.

## The Challenge

AI can now reason about almost anything. What it still lacks is context about the physical world: who is doing what, and what changed since yesterday. A model can read a messy voice note or check a photo against a drawing. But it can’t tell whether the crew about to close a wall has the latest plans.

The Context for the Physical World track is about closing that gap. Within 24 hours, take a swing at catching problems on a construction site before they turn into rework and delays. Or show what people, agents and robots can do once a physical place is understood in real time.

## What You’re Building

Two directions, pick one or combine them:

Catch it before it’s rework: detect a problem on site (a missed change, work out of sequence, a stale drawing) early enough for someone to act on it.

Understand the place in real time: build a live picture of a physical site that people, agents or robots can act on.

## What Great Looks Like

Example directions, drawn from how Talona approaches the problem. Not the only valid ones:

Connect to what the site already uses: chats and email, photos and drawings, the schedule. Nobody has to change how they work.

Keep one live record of the site: what changed, who decided it, what’s waiting on whom.

Flag issues as they happen: a change that never reached the crew, work done out of sequence, a drawing that’s out of date.

Answer questions: crews text the system and get answers from the record, while decisions go to the superintendent.
